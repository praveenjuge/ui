## Why it matters

Hierarchy comes from difference. When a page uses 13, 14, 15, 16 and 17px, none of the steps is big enough to mean anything, and the near-misses look like mistakes. A short scale of sizes that are clearly apart makes the order of a page obvious, and makes every new screen quicker to design and to build.

## How to do it

- Pick a base size for body text, often 16px, and a ratio, like 1.25. Multiply up for headings and down for small print, then round: 13, 16, 20, 25, 31, 39.
- Use four to six of those sizes in a product. Most screens need only three: a heading, the body, and something small.
- Name them as tokens (`text-sm`, `text-base`, `text-lg`) and use only the tokens. A size that isn't in the scale is a decision to make on purpose, not a nudge.
- Reach for weight and color before size. Bold or muted text often makes the difference you need without adding a new size.

## When it bends

A marketing page can have one oversized display line outside the scale, as long as it is clearly a different thing. Inside the product, stick to the scale.
