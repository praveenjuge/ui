## Why it matters

Contrast is what makes text readable: on a cheap screen, at low brightness, in sunlight, or for someone whose eyes are older or weaker than the designer's. Light grey text looks calm and refined in a mockup on a good monitor, and turns into a smudge on a phone outside.

## How to do it

- Body text needs a contrast ratio of at least **4.5:1** against its background (WCAG 2.2, 1.4.3, level AA).
- Large text needs **3:1**: 24px and up, or 18.7px and up when bold.
- The edges of controls, like a field's border, and icons that carry meaning need **3:1** too (1.4.11).
- Check placeholder text and muted helper text; they fail most often.
- Measure, don't eyeball. Browser dev tools show the ratio in the color picker, and WebAIM's checker takes any two colors.
- Put the passing grey in a token, so nobody picks a lighter one by eye. On this site the muted grey is `#6c6c75`, 5.2:1 on white.

## When it bends

Disabled controls, decorations and logos are exempt. For long reading, aim higher: 7:1 is the AAA level for body text.
